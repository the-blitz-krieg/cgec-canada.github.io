Export corridors

Three markets where Canadian businesses win

CGEC operates across three strategically selected corridors: the United Kingdom, the European Economic Area and Asia. Each corridor has a distinct competitive rationale for Canadian exporters, and CGEC maintains active relationships in all three.

United Kingdom European Economic Area Asia
Corridor 1

United Kingdom

Post-Brexit Britain is actively diversifying its supply chains, procurement relationships and trade partnerships. Canadian companies are among the most natural beneficiaries of that realignment.

Why Canada-UK trade now

The Canada-UK Trade Continuity Agreement preserves the preferential access Canadian exporters held under CETA when the UK was an EU member. The UK is simultaneously pursuing deeper bilateral integration with Canada, making this a critical window for Canadian companies to establish anchor relationships before the market matures.

Post-Brexit regulatory divergence is creating specific, time-limited opportunities. UK procurement offices, retailers and institutions are actively replacing EU suppliers with non-EU alternatives. Canadian companies offering comparable quality at competitive prices with no political complication are well placed to capture this demand.

EICBI parliamentary access

CGEC members benefit from EICBI's 13-year presence at the UK Parliament. Through this relationship, CGEC can facilitate introductions to parliamentary committees, UK government departments, NHS procurement offices and the major financial regulatory bodies including the Financial Conduct Authority.

Sector opportunities

Healthcare Technology

NHS Digital and the broader NHS procurement framework represent one of the world's largest single institutional buyers of healthcare technology. Canadian healthtech companies have a strong track record in NHS procurement.

Financial Technology

London remains the global fintech capital. The FCA's regulatory sandbox is one of the most open environments in the world for testing financial products, and Canadian fintech companies have repeatedly used it to validate products before broader deployment.

Agri-food and Beverage

UK retailers are actively diversifying away from EU agri-food suppliers. Canadian producers with clean-label, sustainably sourced products have a compelling narrative and a clear quality positioning advantage.

Clean Technology

The UK government's net-zero commitment by 2050 requires a sustained multi-decade investment in clean technology procurement. Canadian cleantech companies with proven solutions are well positioned to enter UK supply chains through CGEC introductions.

Corridor 2

European Economic Area

CETA, the Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement, is one of the most ambitious trade deals ever negotiated. Yet most of its potential for Canadian exporters remains unrealised. CGEC exists to change that.

CETA in practice

CETA covers 98 percent of Canadian tariff lines with the European Union. It includes provisions on mutual recognition of professional qualifications, government procurement access across EU member states, and investor protections. The agreement has been provisionally in force since 2017.

Despite these provisions, Canadian goods and services represent a fraction of European import volumes. The barrier is not tariffs but relationships: access to buyers, procurement networks and institutional channels that most Canadian companies simply do not have. CGEC and EICBI provide those channels.

European Parliament access

EICBI is an accredited forum of the European Parliament and registered on the EU Transparency Register at number 423661050520-44. This accreditation gives CGEC members genuine access to European Parliament committees, European Commission policy consultations and EU agency procurement processes.

Priority sectors

EU Green Deal

The European Green Deal is the world's most ambitious climate policy framework. It requires massive procurement of clean technology, critical minerals and sustainable agri-food products. Canada is one of the few countries that can supply all three at scale.

Critical Raw Materials

The EU Critical Raw Materials Act identifies Canada as a preferred strategic partner for mineral supply. CGEC can facilitate introductions to European buyers and processors across the clean energy minerals supply chain.

Horizon Europe

Canadian universities and research organisations can participate in Horizon Europe as associated partners. CGEC can facilitate introductions to European research consortia actively seeking Canadian participation in funded projects.

EU AI Act

The EU AI Act creates a single regulatory framework for artificial intelligence across 27 member states. Canadian AI companies seeking to operate in Europe need a regulatory entry strategy. CGEC provides both the advisory support and the institutional introductions to navigate it.

Corridor 3

Asia

Asia represents the world's most significant long-run growth market for Canadian exports. CGEC's partnership with EICBI, which has maintained a presence in India since 2011, gives CGEC members a 13-year head start.

India: EICBI's home market

India is the world's most populous country, its fifth-largest economy by nominal GDP, and the fastest-growing major economy in the world. The Canada-India relationship spans deep people-to-people ties, an Indian diaspora of nearly 1.8 million Canadians, and a shared interest in technology, clean energy and agri-food trade.

EICBI has convened parliamentary summits in India since 2011 and maintains active relationships with Indian government ministries, industry associations and the major institutional buyers in CGEC's priority sectors. CGEC members access this network directly.

The India opportunity

India's government has committed to 500 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030. Its digital infrastructure, built around the UPI payments network and Aadhaar digital identity stack, is the most sophisticated in the world by population scale. Its pharmaceutical, fintech and technology sectors are among the world's fastest-growing institutional buyers of Canadian products and services.

Key sectors

Renewable Energy

India's 500 GW renewable target by 2030 requires massive procurement of clean technology equipment, grid management software and project development services. Canadian companies have solutions in every part of this supply chain.

Technology

India's technology sector is the world's largest buyer of enterprise software and IT services. Canadian enterprise software companies looking to establish distribution partnerships or enter procurement programmes are a natural fit for the CGEC India programme.

Agri-food

India's growing middle class and its diversifying food import basket create significant opportunities for Canadian agri-food producers, particularly in pulses, canola, seafood and processed food products.

Education and Skills

Canada is one of the world's most respected destinations for international students. The CGEC India programme supports Canadian educational institutions seeking to formalise recruitment, research and credential recognition relationships with Indian counterparts.
